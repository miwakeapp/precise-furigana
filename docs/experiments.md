# Corpus experiments

These experiments compare complete generated databases from the same pinned dictionary release, `3.6.2+20261005200550`. They measure the consequences of individual settings, then inspect changed cases. More segmentation is not automatically better segmentation, and the reviewed samples do not establish corpus-wide accuracy.

## Terminal contraction

Disabling contraction changed 572 of 324,992 records. All 572 became coarser. The change is heavily concentrated: 483 records contain 日本 / にほん and lose the 日 / に boundary. Those 483 records represent propagation of one lexical decision, not 483 independent validations of the heuristic.

I inspected the remaining 89 records, covering 49 distinct changed-span families. Many changes concern conventional abbreviations of on readings, including 蜜柑, 涅槃, 仏蘭西, 桔梗, 吉備, and 滅茶. Others involve unusual spellings, proper names, or readings that deserve individual review. Turning the heuristic off therefore removes useful precision as well as risky precision.

There is a clear counterexample to applying the rule to every character reading: 朔日 / ついたち becomes 朔[ついた]日[ち], also inside 初朔日. The candidate comes from removing final ち from the complete kun reading ついたち of 朔. JMdict independently supplies 朔 as an alternative spelling of the same word; it does not establish a shortened reading ついた. The split is false precision.

| Family | Effect of enabling contraction | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 日本 / にほん, including compounds | 日[に]本[ほん] | Required useful precision. |
| 蜜柑 / みかん, including compounds | 蜜[み]柑[かん] | Plausible conventional on-reading abbreviation. |
| 涅槃 / ねはん, including compounds | 涅[ね]槃[はん] | Plausible conventional on-reading abbreviation. |
| 仏蘭西 / フランス | 仏[フ]蘭[ラン]西[ス] | Useful phonetic transcription boundaries. |
| 両親 / ふたおや | 両[ふた]親[おや] | Plausible numeral morphology; different evidence from on-reading abbreviation. |
| 朔日 / ついたち | 朔[ついた]日[ち] | Unsupported truncation of a whole kun reading. |

Recommendation from this snapshot: retain the useful behavior, but distinguish on-reading contraction from kun-reading morphology. A general deletion of final ち or つ from arbitrary readings is too permissive. Preserving reading provenance allows a narrower rule to be evaluated across the corpus; a whole-word dictionary match alone does not validate its internal character boundaries.

## Global learning from kana-surrounded characters

Enabling the experimental learned-reading lexicon changed 2,711 records: 2,649 became finer, 50 coarser, nine had incompatible boundaries, and three changed only their status. I reviewed a deterministic hash-selected sample of 50 changed records, all nine incompatible records, and all 50 coarser records.

In the 50-record hash sample, 43 changes appeared useful, three were clear regressions, and four remained uncertain. This sample is dominated by finer changes because they dominate the changed population. It cannot measure the total frequency of harmful generalization, and correlated compounds or spelling variants are not independent examples.

Many useful changes recover omitted okurigana or inflected stems: 吸取紙 can split into 吸 / すい, 取 / とり, and 紙 / がみ; 問い合せ窓口 can preserve 問, literal い, 合, literal せ, 窓, and 口; 円盤投 can isolate 投 / なげ. These gains support more work on morphology and evidence from alternate spellings.

The harmful cases demonstrate why inferred readings must not automatically become universally available:

| Case | Anchored result | Result with global learning | Problem |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 俄景気 / にわかげいき | 俄[にわか]景[げい]気[き] | 俄[にわ]景[かげ]気[いき] | Individually attested readings combine into the wrong word structure. |
| 愛想笑い / あいそうわらい | 愛[あい]想[そう]笑[わら]い | 愛[あい]想笑[そうわら]い | An invalid inferred competitor removes a supported boundary. |
| 一突き / ひとつき | 一[ひと]突[つ]き | 一突[ひとつ]き | New readings introduce an equally scored competing segmentation. |
| 河原鳩 / かわらばと | 河原[かわら]鳩[ばと] | 河[か]原[わら]鳩[ばと] | A context-specific 原 reading combines with an unrelated on reading of 河. |
| 藪蚊 / やぶっか | 藪[やぶっ]蚊[か] | 藪[やぶ]蚊[っか] | Kana-context sokuon placement is incorrectly generalized after kanji. |

The source witnesses explain the failures. あいそ笑い / あいそうわらい yields the inferred reading うわら for 笑: the extra long vowel belongs to the preceding expression, so exact prefix stripping does not establish a character reading. ママ子 / ままっこ yields 子 / っこ, and ヤブ蚊 / やぶっか yields 蚊 / っか; these are useful alignments in those kana contexts but should not decide sokuon placement after kanji. 俄 / にわ is inferred from forms such as 俄か; its local usefulness does not license the incorrect compound parse shown above.

Eight of the nine incompatible changes concern this sokuon displacement: 継子, 鬼子, 一人子, 薮蚊, 藪蚊, 甥子, 姪子, and 俺子. The remaining incompatible change is 俄景気. All 50 coarser outputs become `ambiguous`; recording uncertainty is appropriate when the model has competing paths, but introducing an unsupported path is not an improvement. Conversely, grouping can prevent an existing bad split: 敷詰める changes from 敷[し]詰[きつ]める to the unresolved 敷詰[しきつ]める, which avoids asserting the wrong internal boundary.

Recommendation: keep globally learned character readings experimental and off by default. Prefer inference tied to the spelling/reading pair, credible morphology, or independently reviewed evidence. Keep the inferred witnesses available for inspection; do not treat witness presence as sufficient proof of general validity.

## Whether `gikun` should suppress splitting

In the same pinned source, 438 valid spelling/reading pairs consisting of at least two kanji carry the reading-level `gikun` tag. The anchored snapshot splits 17 of them. Blanket grouping would fix or conservatively avoid some questionable splits, but would also discard justified boundaries.

| Tagged pair | Existing useful boundary | Why blanket grouping is inappropriate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 身体中 / からだじゅう | 身体[からだ]中[じゅう] | The irregular unit is 身体, not the entire compound. |
| 障泥烏賊 / あおりいか | 障泥[あおり]烏賊[いか] | Two separately attested units remain identifiable. |
| 耳木菟 / みみずく | 耳[みみ]木菟[ずく] | A regular prefix precedes an irregular unit. |
| 三馬 / さんま | 三[さん]馬[ま] | The reading tag is shared with 秋刀魚, whose orthography behaves differently. |
| 両親 / ふたおや | 両[ふた]親[おや] | The reading is plausibly decomposable despite the tag. |
| 夫夫 / ふうふ | 夫[ふう]夫[ふ] | A novel spelling can deliberately use identifiable readings. |

八百屋 / やおや has no `gikun` tag in this snapshot; it should not be cited as a currently tagged counterexample.

薔薇 / ばら is tagged `gikun`, while KANJIDIC2 lists バ for 薔 and ラ for 薇. [Kanjipedia groups 薔薇](https://www.kanjipedia.jp/kotoba/0003458700) and its [薔 entry does not list バ](https://www.kanjipedia.jp/kanji/0003458400). This is a real source conflict. A pair-specific reviewed decision can resolve this word without discarding boundaries in every other tagged compound.

Recommendation: use `gikun` as a review or uncertainty signal, with attention to smaller irregular units and alternative spellings. Do not impose an unconditional whole-pair boundary restriction based solely on this reading-level tag.

## Pair-specific variant transfer and broader phonology

A later experiment used generator SHA-256 `bc7d0413c0fc4bd51fa0af6df69f8c041eb4078907609a9d2935b6e8b0e9f698`. It restricted terminal contraction by reading provenance, enabled voicing of dictionary-supported compound units, and introduced general kana-mismatch edges. Both snapshots kept `terminalContraction: true`, `phonologicalAlternations: true`, `compoundVoicing: true`, and `learnReadings: false`; they differed only in `variantTransfers`.

| Snapshot | `variantTransfers` | JSON SHA-256 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No variants | `false` | `d479831ecbd4c807c349afe25873afbf0b5a89c05b7dce26d7c99f714bf6fcf1` |
| Initial variants | `true` | `4be1c2abf8c6c1c75551208a7b9e4c017b69f9914752d79be753b37708e38ffb` |

Compared with the earlier anchored snapshot, the no-variants snapshot changed 2,244 records: 1,793 finer, six coarser, 442 incompatible, and three status-only changes. This is an aggregate comparison of several changes, so its counts cannot identify the effect of compound voicing in isolation. All six coarser cases follow the removal of kun-reading contraction: 睦月, 両親, 両形, 朔日, 初鶏, and 初朔日. The two 朔日 cases remove the previously identified false boundary; the remaining four require separate lexical or morphological support if finer splitting is desired.

The compound-voicing examples in a 35-record deterministic finer sample were credible: 花相撲 preserves 花 and 相撲 / ずもう; 腕時計 preserves 腕 and 時計 / どけい; 居心地 preserves 居 and 心地 / ごこち. However, the same aggregate experiment introduced serious false precision through overly general kana-mismatch edges. A 35-record incompatible sample included the following:

| Pair | Problematic result | Why it is problematic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 湯上り / ゆあがり | 湯[ゆ]上[あ]り[がり] | Part of the kanji's omitted okurigana is assigned to an already literal り. |
| 問い合せ窓口 / といあわせまどぐち | 問[と]い合[あ]せ[わせ]窓[まど]口[ぐち] | The missing わ belongs to 合's spelling variant. |
| 穴開パンチ / あなあけパンチ | 穴[あな]開[あ]パ[けパ]ンチ | A Japanese stem remainder is assigned to the first katakana of a loanword. |
| 見出す / みいだす | 見[み]出[い]す[だす] | Available single-character readings overwhelm the actual compound boundary. |
| 果々しい / はかばかしい | 果[は]々[か]し[ばかし]い | The residual assignment creates unsupported repeated-character boundaries. |

These outputs are marked `ambiguous`, but that status does not make the asserted boundaries correct. A valid candidate for an unrelated kanji reading must not justify moving leftover pronunciation onto neighboring kana.

Pair-specific variant transfer changes 5,436 records relative to that no-variants snapshot: 5,090 finer and 346 incompatible, with no coarser or status-only changes. Forty deterministic finer samples showed useful omitted-kana recovery, including 植木鉢, 積重ねる, 漬込む, 仮差押, 夕焼空, and 支払準備率. Forty deterministic incompatible samples mostly repaired the new mismatch problem: 湯上り, 問い合せ窓口, and 穴開パンチ regain the expected boundaries. Some source pairs omit particles; transfer then assigns their pronunciation to the preceding written segment, as in 耳 / みみを within 聞き耳たてる. That is a pair-specific representation of omitted text, not a reusable reading of 耳.

Variant transfer does not eliminate the underlying mismatch risk. Of the 442 incompatible changes introduced by the aggregate rule update, 165 retain the same segmentation after variant transfer. These include 見出す and 果々しい above, as well as 御婆さん / おばーさん yielding 御[お]婆[ば]さ[ーさ]ん. Recommendation: keep the pair-specific transfer, but constrain general kana-mismatch candidates before accepting the aggregate change. The audit prompted additional engine work; this snapshot is retained as an experiment, not declared the final release.

The initial-variants reference comparison reports 227,158 exact, 72 finer, 10,803 coarser, 224 incompatible, 373 uncomparable, and 86,362 absent pairs. Those improved agreement counts coexist with the errors above and therefore must not be described as an accuracy score.

## POS-derived continuative stems

Enabling the constrained continuative feature changes 785 of 324,992 pairs. All 785 become finer; none becomes coarser, acquires incompatible boundaries, changes only status, or is added or removed. The feature derives regular one-kanji verb stems from the licensed source's part-of-speech information and admits them as weak, context-dependent candidates. This provides more specific evidence than globally learning arbitrary character readings from kana-surrounded words.

I inspected a deterministic hash-selected sample of 50 finer changes. All appeared linguistically plausible; examples include 吸取紙 as 吸[すい]取[とり]紙[がみ], 申立 as 申[もうし]立[たて], 成田離婚 as 成[なり]田[た]離[り]婚[こん], and 思出の記 as 思[おもい]出[で]の記[き]. These examples also show that useful morphological evidence can apply to conventional compounds and names, not just visibly inflected verbs. None of the 785 changed pairs carries `gikun` in the pinned JMdict source.

Within the 785 changed pairs, 764 agree with the reference boundaries, 15 are coarser, two incompatible, and four absent from the reference. I inspected all 17 comparable disagreements. The incompatible cases, 熟寿司 / なれずし and 思出の記 / おもいでのき, preserve plausible stem boundaries that the reference places elsewhere. The 15 coarser cases retain unrelated grouped units such as 烏帽子, 文字, 提灯, and 縮緬. No clear regression was identified in this inspection. Recommendation: enable continuative stems by default while keeping global inferred readings off. The finite review supports that decision; it is not a proof of correctness for every affected word.

The complete comparisons are saved locally as `constrained-changes.jsonl` and `continuative-changes.jsonl` under `.cache/experiments/`. The continuative reference comparison yields 227,476 exact, 48 finer, 10,590 coarser, 143 incompatible, 373 uncomparable, and 86,362 absent pairs. Final-release metrics should always be tied to the final output hash, since adopting a default or updating implementation files also changes recorded generator provenance.

## Final vowel-extension guard

An independent adversarial review found a limitation outside the deterministic samples: vowel extension could consume the first mora of a following kanji. In the continuative snapshot above, 説き出す / ときいだす assigns きい to き and だ to 出; 染め出す / そめいだす does the same with めい. 届け出伝染病 / とどけいででんせんびょう assigns けい to け, and 呉の母 / くれのおも assigns のお to の. The actual kanji readings begin with the vowel that the rule consumed.

I inspected all 75 records in that snapshot containing a one-kana-to-two-kana phonological expansion. Most were legitimate long-vowel spellings, particularly katakana loanwords. One additional problematic composition appeared in 機内エンターテイメント / きないエンターテインメント: expanding テ to テイ and then substituting イ with ン invented a dubious internal correspondence.

The final rule therefore restricts omitted written vowels to katakana, while allowing an explicit prolonged mark ー after either script. Any following written kana must still match literally after the extension. The implementation agent rescanned all distinct source pairs and inspected all 57 remaining extension records. The conservative restriction sacrifices some useful hiragana variants, including めんど臭い / めんどうくさい, to avoid moving pronunciation across an adjacent kanji boundary. Exact final-build consequences are recorded below rather than inferred from the earlier sample.

A subsequent finite interpretation of dotted KANJIDIC okurigana restores useful precision without vowel stealing: for い.だす, 出す can receive 出 / いだ plus literal す when that suffix matches the attested spelling and reading. The implementation agent's complete comparison found 18 finer pairs and no coarser or incompatible changes; I independently inspected all 18. They include 見出す, 説き出す, 染め出す, 上框, 桁上り, 充満る, and 臑を嚙る. The changes are credible and preserve literal kana.

However, the aggregate release-candidate comparison uncovered a separate residual-anchoring problem. Relative to the continuative snapshot, it changed 37 records: 15 finer, 11 coarser, and 11 incompatible. Inspection of every changed record found six incompatible regressions: めんど臭い acquired 臭 / うくさ; かっこ良い acquired 良 / うい; 日おい acquired 日 / ひお; あいそ笑い and あいそ笑 acquired 笑 readings beginning with う; and ちょうちょ結び acquired 結 / うむす. Removing a vowel-extension candidate allowed the literal anchors of a whole-word edge to assign the leftover vowel to an adjacent kanji. That is an unsupported boundary, not a conservative grouped result.

This intermediate candidate has JSON SHA-256 `a038a0dcda52b7d3162759f16d6e34b72770c20a31e555a2072d06aaffdb8d50` and generator SHA-256 `570fe2e52822c42805dce454bdb962dd5ae2eb67904f106494bb9cc8eb8538f4`. Its reference counts improve to 227,497 exact, 48 finer, 10,580 coarser, and 132 incompatible, with 373 uncomparable and 86,362 absent. Those numbers nevertheless conceal the six regressions because several agree with the reference's own problematic residual assignments. The full delta is saved as `.cache/experiments/final-changes.jsonl`, and this snapshot as `.cache/experiments/vowel-restriction-initial.json`. The audit therefore did not accept this candidate as final.

## Initial accepted local build

Comparing the complete corrected build against the intermediate candidate above changes exactly the six identified pairs, all to conservative whole-word groups. There are no other segmentation or status-only changes. I inspected all six final records. The guard resolves the false boundaries while retaining the documented precision limitation: these particular hiragana spelling variants still need stronger evidence for a finer result.

### Evidence and rejected generalizations

- Weak character evidence remains separate from ordinary on/kun readings. It includes nanori, complete dotted kun readings, and source-derived readings with a leading kanji witness. A leading kana witness cannot contribute a reusable residual reading, because it can conceal a vowel that belongs to the kana. In an isolated experiment, the combined weak-evidence refinement added boundaries to 2,903 pairs without moving existing boundaries.
- Kana and single-kanji spelling substitutions transfer boundaries only between restricted readings of the same entry. Opaque replacement runs stay grouped. An okurigana guard rejects mappings that assign a preceding character’s omitted suffix to its neighbor. A single-kanji substitution ablation produced 844 finer results and no incompatible or coarser results.
- Reading-length transfers must preserve the independently aligned target boundaries. An earlier experiment incorrectly moved the boundary in 見出す and removed the one in 八咫; both are retained as regressions.
- Literal internal kana can expose phrase structure, but omitted particles require localized uncertainty. A broad particle-prefix guard was rejected because it interfered with ordinary character readings; the accepted guard requires independently attested spelling or reading evidence.
- Phonetic ateji uses bounded on-reading correspondences and exact kun readings, and requires a unique best complete character path. The isolated experiment produced 244 finer results. An independent implementation reviewer inspected all 227 development-safe changed rows without finding a false boundary. Held-out entries were not inspected. This review supports the rule without certifying every affected result.
- A synthetic adversarial integration test demonstrated that a valid transferred short-word split could move earlier boundaries in a longer word. The final pipeline compares the result against its pre-transfer alignment and accepts only refinements.

The 16 incompatible changes chiefly repair the allocation of shortened kana vowels to adjacent kanji. Every development-safe incompatible change was inspected; examples include ゆう然, けい紙, もう一度, ジェリー缶, and ディレクトリー名. Held-out examples remain quarantined for independent evaluation. Full local changes are retained in `.cache/experiments/superiority-frozen-v3/changes.jsonl`; the filtered development file excludes all 570 withheld entry IDs.

The independent family evaluation is defined in [the frozen protocol](evaluation-protocol.md). Its labels were completed before the candidate outputs were examined. Regression success and reference agreement remain separate from that evaluation.

## Further lexical and spelling evidence

The next combined experiment adds same-entry counter symbols and Latin spellings, per-character Mandarin sound evidence, and two weak contextual on-reading correspondences. Compared with frozen candidate v3, it adds boundaries to 737 pairs, with no coarser, incompatible, or status-only changes. All 103 current regressions pass. Its reference comparison reports 233,091 exact, 217 finer, 4,745 coarser, 204 incompatible, 373 uncomparable, and 86,362 absent records. This is still a development experiment; the frozen blind results above apply to v3 only.

Counter-symbol transfer independently added 142 finer pairs, and Latin sibling transfer added 34 in its subsequent integrated ablation. Sources such as か月 and 霞が関 establish how ヶ, ヵ, and ケ correspond to sound within those words; ウェブ版 establishes Ｗｅｂ[ウェブ]版[ばん]. These rules do not infer readings for unfamiliar symbols or individual Latin letters. An independent reviewer inspected all 126 safe counter rows and all 38 direct Latin prototype rows without identifying a false boundary.

The Mandarin layer uses the KANJIDIC2 pinyin field plus explicitly listed Japanese on-readings inside entries with applicable Chinese source metadata. A broader optional gate requires an all-Han spelling, an all-katakana reading, and a unique complete match using pinyin evidence for every character. That gate establishes a character-sound correspondence, not Chinese etymology: a few Korean-origin spellings also match. The full experiment found 241 direct and 56 propagated refinements; all development-safe direct and propagated changes were reviewed, with an independent second review of the 43 broader-gate additions.

Initial on-reading voicing or semivoicing added 159 finer pairs without other changes in isolation. Contextual terminal nasal loss added 103 finer pairs, but also destabilized 24 older results through downstream transfers. The adopted integration therefore compiles a protected pipeline with both new sound correspondences disabled and accepts only refinements of its complete result.
